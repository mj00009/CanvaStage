# Licensing and trademarks

This document summarizes the repository’s current licensing state and a recommended future change. It is not legal advice.

## Current license

CanvaStage is currently distributed under the GNU General Public License version 3 (GPL-3.0), as stated in the repository’s `LICENSE` file.

GPL-3.0:

- permits anyone to use the software without a license fee;
- permits copying, modification, and redistribution;
- requires distributed derivative works to remain under GPL-compatible copyleft terms and makes corresponding source obligations part of binary distribution;
- disclaims warranties and limits contributor liability.

The repository is not currently MIT-licensed.

## Recommendation: Apache License 2.0

Apache-2.0 better matches the stated goal of allowing broad free use without requiring downstream applications to adopt the same license. Compared with MIT, Apache-2.0 also provides:

- an explicit patent license and patent-termination provision;
- an explicit statement that the license does not grant rights to trade names, trademarks, service marks, or product names;
- detailed warranty and limitation-of-liability terms;
- standard notice-retention rules.

A custom license that mentions Canva is not recommended. Custom wording increases ambiguity, may no longer qualify as open source, and cannot remove Canva’s trademark rights.

Relicensing should happen only after confirming that the copyright owner has authority over every contribution. Existing GPL copies remain available under the license that accompanied them; changing a future release does not revoke those grants.

References to Canva describe compatibility and the service with which the software interoperates. The software license does not grant permission to use Canva’s name, logos, trade dress, or other branding. Users, modifiers, and distributors are responsible for complying with Canva’s current terms and trademark requirements.

Canva’s published integration guidance permits descriptive wording such as “for Canva,” “for use with Canva,” “works with Canva,” or “compatible with Canva,” and warns against implying that an integration is made, endorsed, or certified by Canva. Canva’s developer terms state that use of Canva branding is prohibited unless authorized.

Because the current product name contains “Canva,” the lowest-risk long-term branding approach is to adopt a distinct primary name and describe it as “for Canva” or “compatible with Canva.” A disclaimer is useful, but it does not by itself authorize a third-party trademark or eliminate claims by its owner.
